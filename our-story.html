Our story.

The work came before the paperwork. A middle-schooler started making things for others — years later, her family built a nonprofit to carry that work further.

The founder

It started at the kitchen table.

Siya started making things for others in middle school — first blankets and toys for shelter animals, then comfort gifts for the people who care for cancer patients. No organization, no logo, no plan beyond the next batch. Just the belief that handmade comfort, delivered in person, matters.

As the work grew, so did the need for something sturdier behind it: a legal home for the programs, a way for donations to be tax-deductible, a structure volunteers could rely on. So the family formalized what she had already built. In June 2025, Kindroots Foundation was incorporated as a California nonprofit. In November 2025, the IRS recognized it as a 501(c)(3) public charity.

Today Kindroots runs two programs — Share & Care, making comfort eye pillows for cancer-care and hospice teams, and Treats for Tails, making blankets and toys for shelter animals. The work still starts the same way it always did: someone making something, by hand, for someone who needs comfort.

Timeline

Every date below is real.

No backstory invented after the fact. These are the milestones as they happened.

Summer 2023

Treats for Tails begins

Siya, finishing 8th grade, starts making blankets and toys for shelter animals — the first handmade comfort project, run from home.

Summer 2024

Share & Care begins

Finishing 9th grade, she starts a second project: handmade comfort gifts for cancer-care teams and the people who look after patients.

June 2025

Kindroots Foundation incorporated

The family formalizes the work as a California nonprofit public benefit corporation, giving both programs a permanent legal home.

November 17, 2025

IRS 501(c)(3) determination

Kindroots is recognized as a tax-exempt public charity. Donations to the foundation are tax-deductible.

October 3, 2026

Eye pillows delivered to Hospice of the East Bay

Comfort eye pillows delivered to the hospice clinical team in Pleasant Hill — the program's first hospice delivery.
